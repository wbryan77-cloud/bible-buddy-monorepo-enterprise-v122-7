## Teaching method to preserve
1. Read explicit text.
2. Slow down on decisive wording.
3. Convert wording into a concrete observation.
4. Ask the obvious-but-often-missed question.
5. Separate observation from inference.
6. Expand line-upon-line across Scripture.
7. Surface the strongest countertext.
8. Use original language/history only when needed.
9. Classify confidence before rendering an answer.

Example from founder-described livestream method: women arrive at the tomb and Jesus is already absent. `DIRECTLY_SUPPORTED`: tomb empty at arrival; resurrection occurred before discovery. `NOT DIRECTLY STATED`: exact resurrection hour. `INFERENCE`: therefore necessarily rose before first day began. `CHRONOLOGY_DISPUTED`: Wednesday burial → Saturday resurrection until full Gospel, feast-calendar, idiom and countertext chain is reconciled.

## Resurrection chronology evidence gate
Direct chain: Jonah 1:17; Matt 12:40; Matt 16:21; Matt 27:62–64; Mark 8:31; Luke 24:7,21,46; John 19:31; John 20:1; 1 Cor 15:4.
Counter/idiom comparison: Esther 4:16–5:1; 1 Sam 30:12–13 and other ancient reckoning examples.
Required questions: burial marker; preparation; annual/high Sabbath; weekly Sabbath; spice purchase/preparation; first-day visit; three-days/three-nights; third-day; after-three-days language.
Never stamp Daniel 9:27 as a weekday; `midst of the week` is direct wording, but a Wednesday-crucifixion application is disputed inference.

## Sabbath evidence gate
Direct chain: Gen 2; Ex 16/20/31; Isa 56/58/66; Matt 12; Mark 2; Luke 4; Matt 24:20; Acts 13/16/17/18; Heb 4; Revelation commandment language.
Required transition/countertexts: Acts 20:7; 1 Cor 16:2; Rom 14; Col 2; Rev 1:10; first-day resurrection appearances.
Teaching question: Where does the text explicitly change the seventh day to the first day? Follow-up guardrail: absence of one explicit change verse does not by itself settle every covenant/application question.

## Holy days / Passover / Easter
Leviticus 23 controls the calendar vocabulary. Trace Gospel/Acts/Paul occurrences and Zech 14. Occurrence after the cross is evidence of continued biblical/cultural presence, not by itself a full command argument. Historical Easter claims require source-by-source verification; do not flatten Bede/Eostre, Ostara, eggs, rabbits, Constantine, Nicaea, or Sunday history into slogans.

## Dietary law / Acts 10
Acts 10 directly records Peter's vision and Peter's explanation that God showed him not to call any **man** common or unclean. This directly establishes the people/Gentile application. It does not by itself settle the entire dietary-law continuity question. Required chain: Gen 7; Lev 11; Deut 14; Isa 65–66; Mark 7; Acts 10–11; Rom 14; 1 Tim 4. Mark 7:19 requires Greek/syntax review before slogan use.

## Soul / spirit / death
Mandatory lexical check: Hebrew nephesh/ruach; Greek psyche/pneuma. Do not casually substitute soul, spirit, body, person, life, or breath. Strong chain: Eccl 9; Job 14; Dan 12; John 3:13; John 11; 1 Thess 4; 1 Cor 15; Rev 20. Required difficult texts: Luke 16; Luke 23:43; 2 Cor 5; Phil 1; Rev 6; transfiguration.

## Father / Son / Spirit
Keep Father/Son explicit-text chain; test Jesus' preexistence, Word language, divine titles/authority, prayers to Father, Father-greater statements. Do not flatten Holy Spirit into `just an angel` without full John 14–16; Acts 5; Matt 28:19; 2 Cor 13:14; Spirit-of-God; messenger/angel; Gabriel; and original-language analysis.

## Kingdom / heaven / earth

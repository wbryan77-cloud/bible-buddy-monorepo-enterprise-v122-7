# Volume 1 — Story Design System

**Date:** 2026-08-29  
**Principle:** Inside the story, the reader should forget about design.

Stories are literary composites informed by real kinds of counseling encounters, struggles, mistakes, and consequences. Design protects humanity; it does not decorate emotion.

**No character portraits. No story illustrations unless Founder later requests.**

---

## Standard narrative treatment

| Element | Spec |
|---|---|
| Body | Same body serif as teaching prose |
| Paragraph | First-line indent; no extra space between consecutive story graphs except scene breaks |
| Scene break | Single centered ornament *or* 1 blank line + no ornament (prefer blank in grief chapters) |
| Dialogue | Standard quotation marks; no speech balloons; no bold for every spoken line |
| Interior thought | Italics sparingly; do not italicize whole paragraphs |
| Time stamps / props | Remain in sentence flow — do not badge “2:17 a.m.” as UI |

---

## Opening-story treatment

- Chapter opens in story whenever manuscript does (most chapters).
- No callout, no tip box, no Scripture box above first story beat.
- Chapter number + title only above; then air; then story.
- First 1–2 pages: **story immersion zone** — suppress furniture chrome.

## Return-to-story treatment

- “Story return” H2 may be visually quieter than teaching H2s (small caps / rule / reduced size) so return feels like landing, not a new lecture.
- Prefer page-turn *into* return when emotional payoff is high (Maya close, Lena dish, Denise table).

## Dialogue treatment

- Keep conversational rhythm of manuscript.
- Do not pull every memorable line into a quote card.
- Exception candidates: Denise “We’re still us under this”; Ava loneliness question; Lena peak triad — and even those may be **isolated lines**, not cards.

## Emotional-pause treatment

| Trigger | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Disclosure of shame/grief/fear | Extra leading or blank line after; no icon |
| Before major teaching H2 after story | Prefer page-turn or generous space |
| After peak line | Isolate; consider verso blank in print (Founder/prototype) |

## When no callout is better

Default for stories: **no callout**.

Never place callouts on:

- Lena’s grief beats
- Uncle’s Romans 8:28 sticker moment (the contrast *is* the design)
- Maya pharmacy scare
- Any death/grief disclosure
- Any sexual-dignity counsel in ch05 (keep sober prose)

## Character continuity (editorial, not illustrated)

### Maya (flagship spine)
- Continuity via **rhyme**: grocery / dairy / cracked spine / unfinished tabs / kitchen counter.
- ch00 and ch12 share opener quietness and closer quietness.
- Do not badge “Maya returns.”

### Lena (protect)
- Quietest visual register in the book.
- Peak line *“I’m still here. It’s still hard. God is still God.”* → **evaluate restraint first**.
  - **RECOMMENDED:** isolated line, same size or +1 pt, generous space above/below; optional soft rule; **not** giant pull-quote poster.
  - Giant pull quote only if Phase 2 prototype proves restraint feels underpowered (Founder decides).

### Nora vs Lena
- Design must never merge identities: Nora = discipline inch; Lena = grief hinge.

### Named-frame stories
When a chapter’s named frame arrives after story (Beloved before brand, Steward’s table, etc.), the frame may use the **single** Key Idea callout — once — then return to prose.

---

## Success test

If a reader describes the design of a story page, the system failed. If they describe Maya, Lena, or Owen, it succeeded.

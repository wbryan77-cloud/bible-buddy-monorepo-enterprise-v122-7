# Story Brief — Prayer that tells the truth

- **topic:** Honest prayer vs performance prayer; lament and petition under God  
- **reader archetype:** Person who can “pray well” in public and freeze in private; tired of spiritual theater  
- **setting:** Church lobby after a polished midweek service; then a parked car; later a quiet kitchen at night  
- **surface problem:** “I don’t know what to say to God anymore”  
- **deeper wound:** Fear that God only accepts impressive, tidy faith; shame about anger, doubt, numbness, need  
- **cultural advice:** Only pray positive declarations; keep a faith aesthetic; “don’t confess weakness”; treat prayer like content  
- **misconception:** Honest lament is unbelief; the Pharisee’s long prayer is the model; silence means God left  
- **escalation:** Gives a fluent public prayer that impresses people; drives home and cannot say one true sentence alone; almost quits praying because prayer feels fake  
- **cost of continuing:** Either hollow performance or bitter silence  
- **choice point:** Trade performed spirituality for truth-telling before the throne of grace  
- **biblical question:** What does Scripture teach about approaching God honestly—lament, Lord’s Prayer, High Priest, Pharisee/publican?  
- **first action:** One unperformed prayer (3–7 true sentences) within 24–72 hours; optionally one lament Psalm read aloud  
- **unresolved realism:** Feelings may not lift immediately; prayer may still feel awkward  
- **emotional landing:** Permission to be honest without calling honesty rebellion  
- **callbacks (light):** Optional glance at Camila’s “I don’t want to pretend” Tuesday prayer; do not redo mind-chapter anxiety arc  
- **protagonist:** Naomi (original; may recur lightly elsewhere)  
- **named frame (max 1):** The prayer that tells the truth

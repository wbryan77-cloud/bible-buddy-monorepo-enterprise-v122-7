# Chapter — The person you’re becoming

(Gate 8 controlled draft — expand to full freeze as `04_FULL.md`)

Tessa stood in front of the closet like it was a witness stand.

Black blazer or softer sweater. The networking event wanted “future founder energy.” On her phone, a note titled *Identity 4.0* listed verbs she had borrowed from podcasts: *Become decisive. Become magnetic. Become the kind of person who…* She had deleted 3.0 two months earlier after it stopped working.

At the event she performed well enough. In the rideshare home she felt the familiar crash: *I still don’t know who I am. I only know how to launch versions.*

Someone she loved once asked, kindly, “What’s underneath the rebrand?” Tessa had laughed so she would not cry.

*Can I become someone under God without inventing myself as a product?*

---

## What are you really asking?

Under identity fog often sit:

- Am I only as real as my latest story about myself?  
- If I fail the new me, am I fake?  
- Is purpose a brand?  
- Does God meet the unfinished person?

Light cross-link: Malik’s kitchen nights named anxiety and aim together; this chapter goes after the identity project itself—not a Malik redo.

---

## You’ve probably heard this before

Self-story shapes behavior—**THEMATIC_PARALLEL**. Identity-as-self-salvation and purpose-as-product contradict Scripture’s gift of image and new creation in Christ.

---

## What Scripture actually says

Genesis 1: image before self-invention. Romans 12:1–2: mercies → living sacrifice → transformed mind. Ephesians 4:22–24: put off / put on in Christ-learned life. 2 Corinthians 5:17: new creature in Christ. Reject self-help identity as rival gospel.

---

## Named frame

**Beloved before brand.**

---

## What this does not mean

Not anti-growth, anti-vocation, or anti-therapy. Not “do nothing.” Not that tactics never matter—only that they are not first.

# Chapter — What God is like from Genesis to Revelation

(Gate 8 controlled draft — expand to full freeze as `04_FULL.md`)

Owen left the dinner with a paper plate of leftover pasta and a sentence he could not answer.

Someone had asked, casually, *So what is God like—to you?* He had smiled, stalled, and talked about “mystery” until the conversation moved on. In the car he felt the shame: not unbelief exactly—confusion dressed as maturity.

Podcasts had given him collage-gods. Childhood had given him a sky examiner. A coworker had given him “the universe.” He wanted the real God—and feared being graded for not already knowing Him.

---

## What are you really asking?

Under “I don’t know what God is like” often sit:

- Is God soft or severe?  
- Can I trust mercy without canceling holiness?  
- Is Jesus optional garnish?  
- Must I finish a system before I may begin?

---

## You’ve probably heard this before

Many traditions speak of a high God or moral order—**THEMATIC_PARALLEL** at most, English-witness only. Do not claim borrowing. For Qur’anic Arabic claims: **ORIGINAL_LANGUAGE_SOURCE_MISSING**. Soft “energy god” and harsh “examiner-only god” both **CONTRADICT** Scripture’s self-revelation.

---

## What Scripture actually says (G2R sketch)

Creator (Gen 1) → holy/loving self-name (Exod 34) → covenant-keeping → judges evil → merciful → Word made flesh (John 1) → crucified/risen → coming King (Rev). Context required each beat. OL: *ḥesed* if load-bearing.

---

## What this does not mean

- Not a systematic theology dump.  
- Not “any god will do.”  
- Not holiness without mercy, or mercy without truth.

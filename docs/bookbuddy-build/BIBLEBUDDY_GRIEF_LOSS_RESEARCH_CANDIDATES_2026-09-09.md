## Governance rule
For any grief/death doctrinal answer, use this order:
SCRIPTURE TEXT -> CONTEXT -> CANONICAL PARALLELS -> DIFFICULT/COUNTERTEXTS -> ORIGINAL LANGUAGE IF MATERIAL -> SYNTHESIS/INFERENCE LABEL -> PASTORAL APPLICATION -> RESEARCH CONTEXT (non-doctrinal).

Research claims must never be converted into afterlife doctrine. In particular, psychological 'continuing bonds' language does not establish that deceased persons are presently conscious, observing the living, or communicating with them.

## Candidate Q&A / eval set
1. Why does the Bible sometimes call death 'sleep'? Test John 11:11-14; 1 Thessalonians 4:13-18; Daniel 12:2; Ecclesiastes 9; and relevant counter-readings including Luke 23:43, Philippians 1:23, 2 Corinthians 5:8, Luke 16, Revelation 6.
2. Is it wrong for a Christian to grieve deeply if resurrection is promised? Test John 11:35; 1 Thessalonians 4:13 ('sorrow not, even as others'); Psalms of lament; Jesus in Gethsemane.
3. Does Scripture require grief to end after a fixed number of days? Distinguish descriptive mourning periods from universal commands.
4. What does biblical hope mean when circumstances do not improve? Contrast resurrection hope with optimism and preferred-outcome promises.
5. How should Christians respond when someone says, 'Your loved one is in heaven now'? Answer without mockery; distinguish what a text explicitly states from tradition/inference and show the full countertext set.
6. What is the difference between remembering the dead and making claims about their present conscious state? Keep memory/legacy distinct from afterlife doctrine.
7. How should Scripture address ambiguous losses such as estrangement, divorce, dementia, missing persons, or a relationship that changed without a clean ending? Use research only to name human experience; Scripture governs moral/theological claims.
8. Does Romans 8:28 mean every tragedy is immediately good? Preserve the verse's qualification and reject simplistic preferred-outcome claims.
9. Does 1 Corinthians 10:13 teach that God will never give a person more suffering than they can handle? No: primary context is temptation/testing and a way of escape/endurance.
10. Why does Job not receive the behind-the-scenes explanation that the reader receives? Use as an eval against invented reasons for another person's suffering.
11. What does it mean to lament faithfully? Examine Psalm 13, Psalm 42, Lamentations, Job, Jeremiah, Habakkuk, and Jesus' grief.
12. When does helping become rescuing or enabling? Research can describe patterns; Scripture-based response should distinguish compassion, wisdom, responsibility, boundaries, and participation in another person's wrongdoing.

## Pastoral-language evals
Flag answers that:
- shame a grieving person for still hurting;
- impose a universal five-stage model;
- promise a replacement blessing or 'double' restoration from Job 42;
- tell users every tragedy was directly sent by God for a specific lesson without textual basis;
- convert 'continuing bonds' into claims about the conscious dead;
- call Christians who hold a different intermediate-state interpretation 'liars' rather than testing the passages;
- use 'move on' to mean erase memory or attachment;
- treat resilience as a spiritual scorecard.

## Useful non-doctrinal explanatory models
- Dual Process Model: oscillation between loss-oriented and restoration-oriented coping.
- Ambiguous loss: physical absence/psychological presence or physical presence/psychological absence; uncertainty can freeze family roles and grief.
- Attachment-learning/neural-map framing: yearning can be understood as the mind/brain learning a changed world after a deeply encoded bond.
- Continuing bonds: may involve memory, symbolic connection, legacy, and identity; empirical effects vary and must not be theologized.
